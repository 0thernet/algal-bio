# Pitfalls, Hypothesis Pattern, and Negative Control

**Framing note:** I have not fetched or inspected these GEO records; the dataset descriptions are taken as given in the prompt. Everything below is general methodological reasoning about how one *would* approach such an integration, not a report of analyses performed or results obtained.

## Four Key Pitfalls

**1. Mismatched resolution and normalization between bigWig signal and RNA counts.**
LMNB1 z-score bigWigs are smoothed, condition-aggregate tracks — already averaged across replicates and likely across a window function. RNA-seq counts are gene-level, discrete, and replicate-resolved. Directly overlaying "LMNB1 increase at locus X" with "RNA decrease at gene X" risks a resolution mismatch: a broad LAD-domain-scale z-score bump may not correspond cleanly to a single gene's TSS/body, and smoothing can shift or blur apparent peak boundaries relative to annotated gene coordinates. Any locus-level call needs an explicit rule for how bigWig signal is aggregated over a gene interval (mean, max, promoter-proximal window) and that choice should be justified and reported, not left implicit.

**2. Z-score bigWigs confound signal magnitude with variance structure, and "aggregate" likely erases replicate/biological variance entirely.**
Because these are z-scored and condition-aggregated (not per-replicate), there is no visible confidence interval on the LMNB1 signal — a locus could show an apparent siScr→siLMNA z-score increase driven by a change in the reference distribution used for scoring rather than a true absolute signal change. Without access to raw or replicate-level bigWigs, statistical significance of "relative LMNB1 increase" cannot be established, only descriptive ranking. Treating a ranked z-score difference as if it were a hypothesis-tested effect is a common overreach.

**3. Correlation between LADs and transcription is confounded by pre-existing chromatin state, not necessarily causal to LMNA depletion.**
Genes near the nuclear periphery are already lowly expressed at baseline in most contexts; LMNB1 enrichment and low RNA often co-occur independent of any acute perturbation. To claim a *relative* LMNB1 increase coupled to *reduced* RNA is mechanistically interesting, the baseline (siScr) state must be considered — loci that were already LMNB1-high and low-expressed offer little new information versus loci that gain LMNB1 signal *and* lose expression specifically upon LMNA depletion (siScr→siLMNA delta in both tracks). Failing to require a baseline-relative comparison inflates false hits toward constitutively lamina-associated genes with no depletion-dependent change.

**4. Cross-study directionality corroboration (H222P vs. isogenic) is confounded by different cell states, disease mechanism, and platform/normalization.**
GSE304575 (LMNA H222P cardiomyopathy mutant vs. corrected isogenic) is a *distinct* perturbation — a missense mutation altering lamin A/C protein function/localization — not an acute knockdown. Using it to "corroborate direction" assumes that H222P and siLMNA produce convergent transcriptional effects on the same loci, which is a testable but unproven assumption, not a given. Differences in differentiation protocol, time in culture, disease-relevant remodeling, and RNA-seq processing pipeline between the two GEO series could all produce spurious concordance or discordance unrelated to shared lamina biology. Any "corroboration" should be treated as weak, hypothesis-generating support (e.g., consistent sign of log-fold-change for a candidate gene set) rather than validation, and effect sizes/statistics from the two studies should not be pooled or directly compared quantitatively.

## Falsifiable Locus-Level Hypothesis Pattern

For a candidate gene set: genes that (a) show significant RNA downregulation in siLMNA vs siScr (GSE300197 counts, standard differential expression), (b) show a positive delta in LMNB1 z-score at the gene body/promoter between siScr and siLMNA aggregate tracks, and (c) show a concordant-direction (down) log-fold-change in H222P vs isogenic-corrected cardiomyocytes (GSE304575) — this set should be enriched, relative to expressed genes genome-wide, near the nuclear periphery/LAD boundaries and should NOT be enriched for genes already showing large baseline (siScr) LMNB1 signal with no depletion-induced change. This is falsifiable: absence of enrichment, or enrichment driven entirely by baseline-high-LMNB1 genes, would refute the pattern.

## Negative Control

Use genes with **stable RNA expression** (no significant change siScr vs siLMNA) and **stable LMNB1 signal** (no delta between conditions) matched for baseline expression level and baseline LMNB1 status to the candidate set. This control tests whether the RNA–LMNB1 delta relationship is specific to the candidate loci or reflects generic noise/normalization artifacts in the z-score tracks. A second useful control is the **DNKASH condition contrast** already present in the design (siScr/DNKASH vs siLMNA/DNKASH) — since DNKASH disrupts LINC-complex nuclear positioning, candidate loci should lose their LMNB1-RNA concordance in DNKASH backgrounds if the effect is genuinely LINC/lamina-dependent rather than an artifact of siLMNA knockdown alone.
